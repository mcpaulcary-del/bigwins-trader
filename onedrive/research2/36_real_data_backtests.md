**Conclusions in one paragraph each.**

1. **H1 (drawdown reduction) passes on the spec's registered window and does not pass on the longest history.** At the RND-012 defaults (A$50,000, cash at 0, 10,000 paired stationary-bootstrap paths, mean block 12): on 1990-01..2023-12 L1's maximum drawdown is 19.2% against 39.5% for B2, a 51% relative reduction, p = 0.066 (< 0.10), Ulcer 6.8% against 12.4%. On 1976-07..2023-12 it is 28.9% against 39.5%, a 27% reduction, p = 0.184: the point estimate clears the 25% bar by two points and the test does not reach 0.10. The difference is October 1987, a one-month fall that no monthly rule can avoid and that sets L1's worst drawdown in the long sample.
2. **The whole benefit comes from two slow bear markets, 2000–02 and 2007–09.** In the fast falls (1987, 2020, and February–April 2025 in the holdout) L1 did nothing or sold near the bottom. From 2010-01 to 2023-12 L1 had the same maximum drawdown as B2 (19.2% against 19.1%) and returned 3.02 points a year less with cash at 0 (2.67 less with cash at the RBA rate).
3. **What L1 costs (return difference L1 − B2, points a year).** Before tax, with idle cash earning nothing: -1.30 points a year on 1976–2023 and -0.70 on 1990–2023. With idle cash earning the RBA cash rate: +0.08 and +0.38. After tax (37% + Medicare, regime b, liquidation value): -1.95 and -1.04 with cash at 0; -1.14 and -0.38 with cash at the RBA rate. Report 11's model figure of 1.35 points a year is inside this range.
4. **What idle cash earns matters more than any rule variant.** The gap between cash at 0 and cash at the RBA rate is +1.31 points a year on 1976–2023 and +1.08 on 1990–2023. The spread across all 26 rule variants at a fixed cash assumption is of the same order, and is not statistically distinguishable from noise.
5. **H11 (after-tax wealth ≥ 90% of B2) fails with cash at 0 in every window that contains the 1990s.** Wealth ratio 0.72 on 1990–2023 and 0.43 on 1976–2023 at the RND-012 defaults. With cash at the RBA rate it is 0.89 and 0.61.
6. **No variant survives as an improvement.** The −15% drawdown brake (report 32) made things worse. H16 early re-entry (report 31) added about a tenth of a point with t below 1. The 10% momentum satellite added about 0.2 points a year with t between 0.9 and 1.7. Deflated Sharpe of L1's active return against B2 is 0.006 (cash 0) to 0.22 (cash at RBA rate) with N = 29 trials. PBO across the variant grid swings between 0.16 and 0.97 depending on the cash and lag setting, which means in-sample ranking of variants carries no information.
7. **Growth objective (report 33, card HG2): fail.** With A$5,000 and A$500 a month, after tax, L1 ends with 0.67 of B2's wealth from 1976 and 0.85 from 1990 (cash at 0); L1 is ahead in 37% of rolling 10-year windows. Report 33's proposed default (growth → STATIC live, L1 in paper) is supported.
8. **The rule is not specific to this data.** The same rule, unchanged, on 20 other national markets (local currency, cash at 0, 1975–2023) cut maximum drawdown by a median 34% and by at least 25% in 16 of 20, at a median cost of 0.90 points a year. On US shares 1926–2023 it cut maximum drawdown from 83.7% to 52.7% (p = 0.043) at a cost of 0.86 points a year. Australia alone is among the weakest cases in the set (second-lowest of 21 by drawdown reduction): 10% reduction at a cost of 1.98 points.
9. **Holdout (2024-01..2025-12): uninformative.** L1 never switched. Its return and drawdown equal B2's (36.7% total, maximum month-end drawdown 6.3%). The RND-025 condition (L1 holdout drawdown not above B2's) holds trivially.

## 3. Method

**Rule.** Exactly PRT-12 and PRT-13. On each month-end, per sleeve, on the fee-adjusted total-return index: v1 = 6-month return > 0; v2 = 12-month return > 0; v3 = index above the mean of its last 10 month-end values. Three or two votes → ON; zero votes → OFF; one vote → keep the prior state. State is OFF before the first evaluation and the state machine is replayed from 1975-01. Base weights 40% Australia, 60% global. An OFF sleeve's share is held as cash. One tranche, offset `last`.

**Execution.** Signal at the close of month m; the new weights earn the return of month m+1 ("lag 0", trading at the signal close — one day better than the spec's next-day order) or of month m+2 ("lag 1", a full month late). The truth for the live system lies between the two and close to lag 0.

**Two engines.**

- *Unit-price engine* (robustness grids, bootstraps, placebos, PBO): weights are base × state, rebalanced monthly; cost is 0.138% of every dollar traded (0.088% commission including GST plus 0.05% half-spread, RND-021).
- *Dollar engine* (headline cost, account-size, contribution and tax results; the H1 statistic): whole account in dollars; commission max(A$6.60, 0.088%) per order plus 0.05% half-spread; A$1,000 minimum order (half for transition buys, PRT-17); ±20% relative band rebalance for L1; July rebalance for B2 (PRT-22); contributions invested into the most under-weight ON sleeve once they reach the minimum order. The two engines agree: correlation 0.9984, CAGR gap -0.11 points at a large account.

**Cash.** Every L1 result is given twice: idle cash earns the RBA cash rate, and idle cash earns 0 (the spec's default until open item I9 is answered).

**Tax (approximate; assumptions stated).** Australian resident individual. Marginal rates 32%, 39% and 47% (30%, 37% and 45% plus 2% Medicare). FIFO parcels. Distributions are taxed each year at the marginal rate: the Australian sleeve's measured dividend yield, and an assumed 2.0% a year for the global sleeve. Franking: 75% of Australian dividends franked at a 30% company rate, credit fully usable (also run with no franking). Interest on idle cash is taxed. Regime (a): 50% discount on gains held more than 12 months. Regime (b): the spec's post-1 July 2027 rules as RND-022 describes them — cost base indexed to CPI for parcels held more than 12 months and a 30% minimum rate on gains. Capital losses carry forward. Tax is paid from the account each 30 June, from cash first and then by a costless pro-rata reduction of units (as if distributions were taken in cash to pay it). Results are given as after-tax liquidation value: final value less tax on all unrealised gains. Today's rules are applied to the whole history; the actual law of 1976–1999 (no CGT before 1985, indexation to 1999, no imputation before 1987) is not modelled. Not modelled: foreign withholding tax and offsets, AMIT cost-base adjustments, the 45-day rule, tax-loss selling.

**Statistics.** Drawdown and Ulcer on month-end values. Sharpe is the mean excess return over the RBA cash rate ÷ its standard deviation, annualised. H1 p-value as RND-012: share of 10,000 stationary-bootstrap paths (mean block 12 months, seed 36) in which B2 maximum drawdown − L1 maximum drawdown ≤ 0, resampling paired monthly rows. Also: an asset-level bootstrap that resamples the underlying sleeve and cash returns and re-runs the rule on each path; placebo tests; Newey–West t-statistics (12 lags); Deflated Sharpe (Bailey and López de Prado 2014); PBO by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (Bailey et al. 2017).

**Out-of-sample discipline.** Development data end 2023-12; the arrays passed to every development script are truncated there. The holdout script (section 10) was run once, after all development tables were produced, and changed nothing. One exception is stated where it occurs: the crash table (section 5) shows the February–April 2025 row because the brief asked for it; it is marked HOLDOUT.

**Trials.** N = 29: 26 distinct rule specifications in family L1 (V01–V26, section 7.1) and 3 in family S1 (momentum satellite, section 9). Cash, lag, cost, account-size, tax, window, bootstrap, placebo and other-market runs are battery replications of a specification (VAL-22) and are not counted. The full list is in `backtest_study/out/trials.csv` and appendix A. Self-checks (`s0_tests.py`): 0 causality violations across all 26 specifications when history is truncated at four dates; weights before a date are unchanged when all later returns are altered.

**Differences from a card run under the spec.** French/Bloomberg market returns, not the RND-024 index proxies; no splice to VAS and VGS; one tranche; month-end only (no day offsets, no intra-month data); whole-unit rounding, the sentinel unit, the A$50 cash reserve and PRT-27 carry rules are not modelled.

Maximum drawdown barely discriminates between variants; Ulcer and ΔCAGR do.
- **The return cost depends a great deal on the parameters when cash earns 0**: -3.28 to -0.85 points a year. Variants that spend more time in cash (V06, V08, V10, V13: 64% to 75% invested) cost the most. L1 as specified (83% invested) is in the cheaper third. Slower lookbacks (V04, V05) were cheapest; that ordering is not reliable (section 8).

- **Per-sleeve against whole-portfolio signal (V19):** no better. Keep per-sleeve.
- **H5 (tranches) and H8 (weekly against monthly)** cannot be tested on month-end data.

## 12. Caveats

1. **Index, not ETF.** These are market-return series from French's library (Bloomberg-sourced international data, CRSP for the US), less a flat fee. They are not the S&P/ASX 300 and MSCI World ex-Australia series that VAS and VGS track, and no splice to the live ETFs was possible (Yahoo, Stooq, MSCI and S&P were unreachable). From memory, calendar-year figures agree with the published index returns to within about one to three points (for example 2008: Australia -40.6%, global -23.9% here); that comparison is [unverified] and must be redone from source in the harness.
2. **Gross dividends, no franking in the data.** French's Australian returns include cash dividends only. Franking enters only through the tax model's assumption (75% franked at 30%). Before tax, both L1 and B2 are understated for an Australian holder by about 0.4 × 4% × 0.75 × 0.43 ≈ 0.5 points a year, B2 slightly more than L1.
3. **Global returns are gross of withholding tax** (French's Developed series), whereas VGS's index is net. Overstates the global sleeve by roughly 0.3 points a year for both strategies [unverified].
4. **USD → AUD timing.** RBA 4 pm Sydney month-end against a later close in the USD returns: 2.3% a year of noise, no bias. Results with French-implied FX are in section 7.6. Before December 1983 the exchange rate was managed, so 1976–83 global-sleeve behaviour in AUD is not representative of a floating currency.
5. **Pre-1990 global sleeve is a two-asset blend** with a back-cast US weight (44% in 1975; the true MSCI weight was higher [unverified]). Section 7.6 shows the effect is small. Results from 1990-07 use no splice.
6. **Australia weight removed at a fixed 2.5%**; after 2025-12 the global series includes Australia.
7. **Cash rate.** The RBA interbank rate is not what a retail broker pays. The spec's default is that IBKR pays 0. The 1976–1995 cash rates (often above 10%) flatter the "cash earns RBA rate" line on the long sample; the 1990-on and 2010-on rows are closer to what a cash ETF would add today.
8. **Month-end data.** Intra-month drawdowns (19 October 1987, 23 March 2020, 8 April 2025) are deeper than shown for both L1 and B2. Tranche offsets (H5), weekly evaluation (H8) and a daily brake cannot be tested.
9. **Lag 0 is one day optimistic** (trade at the signal close). Lag 1 results are shown throughout and tell the same story.
10. **Survivorship and look-back.** Australia and the developed world are markets that survived and did well. The 21-market table is the same set of survivors. French's international files are rebuilt from a current Bloomberg database, so constituent history may be revised.
11. **Tax model is approximate** (section 3): today's rules on the whole history, assumed yields and franking, no withholding offsets, no AMIT adjustments, no 45-day rule. Regime (b) is the spec's description of a future law, not enacted detail I could verify. Use the after-tax figures for ranking and rough size, not as tax advice.
12. **Costs in 1976 dollars.** A$6.60 minimum commissions applied to nominal 1976 balances are an anachronism; the constant-size table (section 4.4) is the cleaner statement.
13. **The rule was not discovered on this data, but the literature that suggested it was written on overlapping data** (10-month SMA: Faber 2007; time-series momentum: Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012). Only the last decade or so is free of that, and from 2010 L1 lost. The pre-1926 and 1926–1970 US results pre-date the literature's samples and do support the drawdown effect.
14. **Momentum proxies** are monthly-rebalanced academic portfolios without trading costs, and the Asia-Pacific one is not Australia.
15. **Not a card run.** Nothing here consumes the spec's trial budget or opens its holdout; but anyone who later runs the registered cards has now seen these results. RND-025's "holdout opened once" should be read as already spent for the L1 family on this data: 2024–2025 has been looked at (section 10.3), and showed no switch.

**Answer to the three questions.**

- *Does L1 pass H1-style tests on this data?* Yes on the registered 1990–2023 window (p = 0.066 with cash at 0, 0.045 with cash at the RBA rate; survives a one-month delay; survives the haircut by a hair). No on 1976–2023 (p ≈ 0.18). The drawdown effect itself is supported out of sample by 20 other markets and by US data back to 1926, and by the placebo test at about the 5% level; what is fragile is its size on any one 30-year window.
- *What does it cost?* With idle cash at zero, about 0.7 to 1.3 points a year before tax and about 1.0 to 2.0 after tax; if idle cash earns the cash rate, nothing before tax (L1 was 0.1 to 0.4 points ahead) and about 0.4 to 1.1 after tax. Brokerage is 0.11% to 0.24% a year of that. The cost is paid in most years in which L1 trades and in most five-year periods; the benefit arrives in about one decade in four.
- *Which variants survive?* L1 as specified, and nothing else. Hysteresis and the three-vote blend earn their place on turnover. The brake, graded states, portfolio-level signals, H16 and the momentum satellite do not improve on it by any test here, and the grid cannot rank lookbacks.
