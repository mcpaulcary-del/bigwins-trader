## 0. Answer first

**Nothing in the drivers layer predicts company returns in a way that survives the pre-registered tests. What this means, in one paragraph. A miner's share price moves with its commodity in the same month, and the exposure map measures that well. But the price has already moved by the time the month ends. Knowing that iron ore rose over the last three to twelve months tells you about as much about next month's Fortescue return as Fortescue's own price history already does, and nothing more. This is report 39's index-level finding (commodities do not lead the ASX at a monthly horizon) repeated at company level with 15 drivers and 300 companies. The apparent success of exposure-weighted buy lists after 2016 is the list being full of resource stocks during a resources upswing; the same lists lost three-quarters of their value in 2008 and 2011–2015. The drivers layer should therefore be used to describe and bound risk (how much of the book is one bet on iron ore or on bond yields), not to pick stocks.

## 0.1 Caveats that change how far to trust this

- **Same price data and defects as reports 51 and 53.** Unlicensed third-party prices, missed splits of delisted companies, no dividends, failures paid at zero.
- **Commodity prices are monthly averages.** The World Bank Pink Sheet and the RBA index publish the month's average early in the next month. No free daily series for iron ore, coal, LNG, base metals or gold was reachable. Daily series were used only for Brent, AUD/USD, US yields, the credit spread and the Nasdaq. A lead of days rather than months could not be tested and is not ruled out.
- **Lithium has no usable price series.** FRED and IMF requests returned 404. Lithium is in the static map only and in no tested signal.
- **The China indicator is a revised series.** The OECD leading indicator on FRED is today's vintage, used with a three-month lag. It is not what was known at the time.
- **US equities are the Nasdaq Composite.** FRED carries only ten years of the S&P 500.
- **The "unseen" period has been looked at before** for other signals (reports 51, 53, 55). It is unseen for these 13 specifications, each of which has now used its one look.
- **The static map is hindsight.** It uses 2026 industry groups, name keywords and revenue shares written from memory. Its 2016–2024 result (13% a year) is not evidence.
- **Stated revenue exposure was not read at source.** No company report or segment note was opened. The revenue shares for about 100 majors in `static_map.py` are from memory and marked unverified.
- **A false start is disclosed.** Two aborted development runs printed partial rows before a data defect was fixed (section 3.4).
- **Differences under one point a year are noise** (report 54).

## 1. What was asked, and what was built

A layer that says what moves each company (commodities, currency, rates, credit, China, US shares, news), tests whether any of it predicts returns under the rules of reports 51, 53 and 56, and writes spec text for a "drivers" input to the Big Wins engine and the learning engine. Scope additions from report 61 were applied: betas strictly trailing; a check of whether GDELT history is point in time; a dated list of royalty and critical-minerals policy changes (descriptive); China trade and freight as descriptive series only. Aggregate short interest and the niche-edge tests (LICs, SPPs, spin-offs, index deletions, tax-loss selling) belong to other agents and were not touched.

## 2. The exposure map

### 2.1 Method (fixed before any return was computed)

At month-end t in month m, each company's monthly returns for the 60 months m−61 to m−2 are regressed on the market and the drivers. Every input was published before t. A company needs 36 months. Two estimates are stored:

- **Joint (ridge) beta**: all 16 drivers at once, after removing the market, shrunk halfway to zero. Used by every signal.
- **Single-driver beta and t-value**: market plus one driver. Used for the map and for reading.

Pink Sheet and RBA figures are monthly averages. The regression uses two-thirds of the sum of this month's and next month's change in the average, which recovers the month-end-to-month-end move if prices follow a random walk.

Drivers: iron ore, copper, gold, nickel, aluminium, zinc, Australian thermal coal, Japanese LNG, US wheat (World Bank Pink Sheet); Brent, AUD/USD, US 10-year yield, Baa-less-Treasury spread, Nasdaq (FRED, daily); Australian 10-year yield, OECD China leading indicator (FRED, monthly); RBA commodity price index in SDR (RBA table I2; single-driver only).

### 3.2 Tests

- **Stage A, monthly.** Each month-end: rank IC with next month's return; top 30 less the equal-weight universe; top less bottom tenth; and a rank regression of next month's return on the signal with 12-1 momentum, last month's return and volatility ("beyond own momentum").
- **Stage A, yearly** for the December-review signals, with report 56's volatility-matched placebo.
- **Placebos, 200 draws.** For exposure signals, the same betas with driver changes taken from a date shifted by a random two years or more. This separates "current driver news matters" from "the list is tilted toward certain stocks". For PEER, ten random peers. For the engine, random-score portfolios. For E1, "H80 rank + random rank".
- **Stage B.** The report-55 engine (calendar corrected), costs and tax unchanged, A$25,000 and A$100,000. The copied engine reproduces report 55's v0 (9.4% and 6.3% on development; 1.6% and 4.0% unseen).
- **Periods.** Development: formations 29 December 2000 – November 2015. Unseen: formations 31 December 2015 – April 2024, exits to 31 May 2024, one counted look per specification, made after the development outputs were hashed (`out/dev_hash.txt`; looks logged in `out/holdout_looks.csv` at 12:47 UTC).

### 3.3 Verdict rules

- **PREDICTS** (development): rank IC above zero with t ≥ 2.0, and regression coefficient t ≥ 2.0, and rank IC at or above the 90th percentile of its placebo.
- **CONFIRMED** (unseen): PREDICTS, and on the look rank IC > 0 with t ≥ 1.645, top 30 above the universe, coefficient above zero.
- **TRADABLE**: report 56's gates G2–G5 on development and, unseen, above the index after costs at both sizes and at or above the 75th percentile of random at both.
- **Survivor** = CONFIRMED. Combinations with H80 and H88 (equal-weight ranks only) for survivors only.

### 3.4 Deviations

1. **D1, data-forced, with a disclosed false start.** The XJO series in the panel begins on 23 November 2000, so the "market" in the beta regressions did not exist for 1997–2000 and no beta could be formed before April 2004. Fix: before March 2001 the market is the equal-weight return of the December 2000 top-300 companies. Before the fix, a development stage-A run on 143 months (April 2004 on) printed rows for DM and DM3. The corrected signal file then failed to save on the first attempt and a second 143-month run printed rows for DM, DM3, DM6, DM12, DM_ALL, DM_STATIC and LL1 before it was stopped. Those rows are kept in `out/dev_A_aborted_143month_run.log` (for example DM rank IC +0.019, t 1.5; DM12 +0.032, t 2.6). Nothing but the market series changed between runs. The verdicts are the same on either sample.
2. **D2.** The China indicator for the last window month is not yet published under the declared lag; it is set to the window mean.
3. **D3.** Random-portfolio controls are shared within groups of specifications that score the same names, not drawn per specification. DM_STATIC's stage-A placebo is a random ranking.
4. **News family not run** (section 7). This is the pre-registered fallback, not a deviation.

Trials: 19 planned; 13 run on returns, 3 splits run, 3 news specifications not run. All 19 are counted.

Readings:

1. **Driver momentum is real but redundant.** The rank IC is above nearly every wrong-date placebo, so the current commodity trend does carry information about which stocks do well next month. It is a fifth to a third of H80's rank IC, and once the stock's own 12-month momentum, last month's return and volatility are in the regression its coefficient is indistinguishable from zero. The commodity trend is already in the share price.
2. **The 12-month trend is the best horizon** and the only one whose top 30 clearly beat the universe before costs. It still fails "beyond own momentum" (t 1.4 against 2.0).
3. **Macro drivers make it worse.** Adding yields, credit, China and the Nasdaq lowers every figure.
4. **No one-month lead.** With this month's driver moves the rank IC is +0.007. With moves as published it is negative.
5. **Laggards do not catch up.** GAP3 is strongly negative, and the placebo with the wrong drivers is just as negative. The signal is "minus the stock's own three-month return" with a driver term that adds nothing. Three-month losers kept losing.
6. **E1's high figures are H80's.** The placebo is the same score with the commodity regime taken from the wrong date; E1 is at its 89.5th percentile on rank IC (90 needed).

### 5.3 How to read a development failure followed by an unseen success

Five specifications (DM, DM6, DM_ALL, DM_STATIC, E1) beat the index and nearly every random list on 2016–May 2024 after failing on 2001–2015. Four reasons not to act on that:

1. **The rule was set in advance.** A signal that fails development cannot be a survivor. The alternative is choosing the period that agrees with the result one wants.
2. **The horizons contradict each other.** On development the 12-month trend was best and the 6-month worst (percentiles 88 and 5.5 at A$100,000). Unseen, the 6-month was best (100th) and the 12-month ordinary (44th). A real effect does not swap its best setting between periods.
3. **No whole-list information.** The unseen monthly rank IC is +0.002. All the return is in which 30 names sit at the top, and those are resource-heavy: 10 to 13 of the 30 DM names were materials or energy companies in six of the eight December lists from 2015 to 2022 (`out/posthoc_december_list_resources_count.csv`, a description made after the look, not a trial).
4. **None is statistically distinguishable from the index.** Excess-return t-values are 0.9 to 1.4, except the hindsight static map (2.4). Deflated Sharpe ratios are 0.22 to 0.29 against a bar of 0.95.

**E1 against the engine.** Development: −0.5 and +0.1 points a year after costs, paired t 0.0 and 0.2. Unseen: +7.0 and +3.1 points, paired t 1.3 and 1.0, drawdown 4 and 2 points deeper. Its unseen lists held 11 to 17 resource names in December 2020, 2021 and 2022 against the engine's 1 to 6. The gain is that tilt in those three years. It is the most interesting row in the report and it is one observation.

### 7.3 Is GDELT history point in time?

- **It is not vintaged.** The API answers from the index as it stands on the day of the request. There is no "as known on date X" query.
- **Short-term stability:** the same query ("Fortescue", January 2017 – May 2024) requested at 08:47 and about four hours later returned identical files (0 of 5,408 rows differ). That shows the index is not changing hour to hour. It says nothing about changes over months or years.
- **The monitored universe shrinks.** Total monitored articles per year in the answer: 228 million in 2017, 193 in 2018, 171 in 2019, 138 in 2020, 120 in 2021, 103 in 2022, 68 in 2023. Raw counts are not comparable across years; "Fortescue" falls from 13,669 articles in 2017 to 3,951 in 2023 mostly for that reason. Any attention measure must be a share of the total, and the pre-registered N1 (raw count against its own six-month average) would have been biased downward throughout.
- **Tone** is computed by GDELT's own dictionary at ingestion; whether re-processing has ever changed historical tone could not be checked [unverified].
- **Consequence:** a back-test on GDELT history is at best "as the index stands now". Only a record collected daily from now on, stored with its collection time, is point in time.

## 11. Reasons this could be wrong

1. **Monthly averages hide short leads.** If a commodity move takes days, not months, to reach a small miner's price, monthly-average data cannot see it. That would need daily commodity prices and an event-study design, and a monthly engine could not trade it at A$25,000 anyway.
2. **The beta model is simple.** Ridge on 60 months with 15 drivers is noisy; the five-year stability of most exposures is below 0.4. A map from audited segment revenue could be sharper. The hindsight static map's unseen result hints at that, and it is exactly the kind of result hindsight produces.
3. **"Beyond own momentum" is a strict bar.** A signal that duplicates momentum is not worthless as a description. It is worthless as an addition to an engine that already ranks on momentum.
4. **Lithium, the largest driver of ASX resource returns in 2021–2023, is missing** for want of a price series.
5. **The market series before March 2001 is a proxy** (deviation D1). It touches the earliest betas only.
6. **Sector labels are today's**, so the description of what the lists held undercounts resource names among delisted companies.
7. **The news family is untested**, not rejected.
8. **Parallel agents' trials are not in N.** A larger N raises the bar; nothing here is near it.
