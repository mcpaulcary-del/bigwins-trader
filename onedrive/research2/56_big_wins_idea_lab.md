## 0. Answer first

**Eleven pre-registered specifications were tested: seven new signals and four equal-weight-rank combinations with the H80 score. One passes the pre-declared gates and has external evidence good enough to shortlist: H80 combined with the "frog-in-the-pan" continuity measure. What this means, in one paragraph. The literature's two best price-only ideas that the engine does not already use, avoiding lottery stocks and buying low beta, do not work as buy lists on the ASX top 300 in 2001–2015: their information is in which stocks to avoid, and the engine's low-volatility component already avoids them. Signals that need monthly trading cannot survive A$6.60 brokerage on A$2,500 positions. What remains is one modest refinement inside the momentum family: prefer stocks whose rise came in many small steps. It did not hurt on development data, helped in one configuration and did nothing in two others. It is worth one line in the paper challenger library because it is cheap, has two independent academic studies behind it, and uses different names from the engine about half the time. It is not evidence for changing the engine.

## 0.1 Caveats that change how far to trust this

- **Same data as reports 51 and 53, same defects.** Unlicensed third-party prices, about one in five splits of delisted companies probably missed, no dividends, holiday rows in the calendar, and an XJO series that is stale for much of 2001 (report 54 section 5.3), which flatters every excess-return t-value on 2001–2015 slightly.
- **Fifteen yearly observations.** Each December list is one observation. Report 54 showed that one day more or less in a volatility window moves an A$25,000 result by a full point. Differences under a point a year are noise.
- **The development period is the period on which H80 was already the best of 16 models.** A combination with H80 inherits that. Passing a gate against "H80 + noise" controls for it only in part.
- **Eleven trials on top of 320 earlier ones.** With eleven tries, one or two passes by luck are expected.
- **Primary sources could not be opened.** The session's network allowed web search but not direct access to nber.org, revfin.org, alphaarchitect.com, ideas.repec.org or university sites. Every literature claim is checked against search-result summaries of abstracts and repository listings, not full texts. [2+] means two or more independent listings or papers agree; it does not mean the paper was read.
- **Volume in the panel is not split-adjusted and there are no share counts.** Turnover, illiquidity, share-issuance and capital-gains-overhang signals could not be built honestly and were not tested.
- **Industry is known only for companies still listed.** Industry momentum could not be tested.
- **Card numbers H88–H90 are provisional.** Another agent is writing cards in parallel; check for a clash before registering.

## 1. What was asked

Find stock-selection ideas worth forward-testing on paper, using only 2001–2015 data and academic evidence from other markets, without spending any of the unseen-period budget. Test each alone and, with H80, only through a pre-declared simple rule. Return at most five ideas that have strong external evidence, do not fail on development data and are cheap enough from A$25,000.

Not duplicated: report 51's models M1–M16 as standalone signals, and the 27 variants of rounds 2–5 that the bigwins2 agent is running (review frequency, weights, number of names, universe 200, caps, regime guards, dropping M2, adding M16, the pinned, trend and spike filters, residual momentum in place of M1).

No combination was allowed for L4–L6 (different trading frequency). No other weights, no three-way combinations.

### 3.2 Tests

- **Stage A, before costs.** Each December 2000–2014: rank IC, and the top 30 against the equal-weight top 300 and against XJO. A volatility-matched placebo: 1,000 random 30-name lists with the same spread across volatility fifths, which shows whether a signal is more than a low-volatility tilt.
- **Stage B, the report-53 engine unchanged** except for the score: A$25,000 and A$100,000, costs, tax, whole shares, the industry cap. Controls: 200 random-score portfolios through the same machinery; for the combinations, 200 runs of "H80 rank + random rank"; for L3, 200 random lists drawn from the same momentum-winner third.
- **Evidence ranking fixed in advance**: MAX, BETA, FIP, RSD, REV, SEAS, REC. The shortlist is ordered by this ranking, never by development returns.

### 3.3 Gates

A standalone signal passes if: rank IC and top-30 excess are positive (G1); it beats XJO after costs at both sizes (G2); it is at or above the 75th percentile of its random portfolios at both sizes (G3); cost drag is at most 1.5 points a year at A$25,000 (G4); worst drawdown at A$100,000 is not below −60% (G5); it is at or above the 75th percentile of the volatility-matched placebo (G6).

A combination passes if: it is not more than 1.0 point a year below the engine after costs at both sizes (K1); it is at or above the 75th percentile of the "H80 + noise" runs at both sizes (K2); its drawdown is not more than 5 points deeper than the engine's (K3); cost drag is at most 1.5 points (K4).

Shortlist: passes its gates **and** has external evidence tagged [2+] with at least one non-US or post-publication confirmation.

### 3.4 Deviations

- **Addendum 10.1**, written and hashed after stages A and B were read and before the replications: seven replication checks (eras, June accounts, cost and delisting stresses, paired bootstrap, list overlap, every-month formations, the Sharpe deflation at N = 350) on specifications already run. None could change a verdict.
- **One conservative departure.** The addendum said C3's card would be labelled "development evidence mixed" if C3 trailed the engine by more than 1.0 point at both sizes in the June accounts. It trailed by 0.6 and 1.4. The trigger was not met by its letter. I apply the label anyway, because C3 also sat in the middle of the noise band there.
- No specification was added, changed or re-run. Trials: 11 planned, 11 run.

## 8. What the forward paper test of H88 can and cannot show

C3's monthly return differed from the engine's by 6.4 points a year (standard deviation) at A$100,000 on development data and 8.5 at A$25,000. Halve the development edge of 3.2 points, as the spec does for any backtested figure (VAL-26), to 1.6.

| Evidence standard for "H88 is better than H82" | Years of record needed to expect to reach it, true edge +1.6 points a year | True edge +3.2 |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary one-sided 95% (t ≥ 1.645) | about 44 | about 11 |
| The spec's t ≥ 3.0 | about 147 | about 37 |

[computed; normal approximation]

So the paper record cannot show that H88 beats H82. What it can show in three to ten years: that the rule is built as written; what it costs; that it is not a disaster (the kill margins); whether the difference book has any drift at all; and whether a list that shares only half its names with the engine tells the same story as the engine about the market. The most likely outcome is "indistinguishable from H82", which is a legitimate result and costs nothing but a `paper_sim` line.

## 9. Reasons this could be wrong

1. **The data.** A licensed re-run could move any number here by a point or more. That is why H88 must pass a licensed 2001–2015 replication before it starts.
2. **The slow MAX is not the published MAX.** The literature uses the last month and holds for a month. A one-month MAX exclusion applied to a yearly list was not tested, because it would rarely bind on a list already chosen for low volatility and because inventing it after seeing L1 fail would be tuning. The same holds for beta: Frazzini and Pedersen's result is a leveraged long-short portfolio, not a buy list.
3. **The results-drift proxy is crude.** Real announcement dates might show drift where the highest-volume day of February or August does not. That needs a results calendar the project does not have.
4. **Evidence tags rest on abstracts and summaries**, not full texts (section 0.1). The frog-in-the-pan international evidence is one study reported in several places.
5. **C3's pass may be luck.** One of four combinations cleared the bar; the June replication did not repeat it. The gates were set to answer "does it fail?", not "does it work?".
6. **Fifteen years, one market, one crash.** 2008 dominates every drawdown figure.
7. **I rejected recency on a rule about sources, not on its results.** If the rule is too strict, a real effect is being left in reserve. The cost of that error is small: it is one `paper_sim` line.
