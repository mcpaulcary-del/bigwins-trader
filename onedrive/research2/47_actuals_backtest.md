## Conclusions

1. **The owner's plan is confirmed on actual data.** It ended A$2,707 ahead of the same portfolio at IBKR. About A$550 of that is commission; the rest is cash waiting for the A$1,000 minimum order and allocation drift. With the real A200/BGBL from Jan 2024, A$22,000 became A$27,675 (13.64% a year money-weighted; about 10.9% after tax if sold).
2. **L1 cost about 2.6 points a year and protected nothing.** At A$50,000 with no contributions it returned 8.80% against 11.40% for static, with identical max drawdown. From 2009 the gap is 2.37 points. AAA on idle cash recovers only about 0.2 points, and the after-tax gap is about 2.4 points.
3. **All ten completed L1 round trips since 2009 bought back higher than they sold.** Re-entry was 2.2% to 14.9% above the exit, mean 6.9%. L1 was invested 86% of the time in the Australian sleeve and 90% in the global one.
4. **2020:** every strategy fell 28.1% on daily closes (20 Feb to 23 Mar) and 19.4% on month-ends. L1 sold VAS on 1 Apr 2020 at A$66.11 and rebought on 2 Nov 2020 at A$75.99. Calendar 2020 was -2.6% for L1 against +3.2% for static.
5. **Feb–Apr 2025:** the fall was 13.4% daily (31 Jan to 7 Apr) and 7.0% month-end. No rule traded: VAS fell to 1 vote at end-March and hysteresis held it ON.
6. **Other episodes:**
   - 2022: L1 sold both sleeves on 1 Jul 2022, two weeks after the low, and returned -17.7% against -8.6%.
   - 2015–16: L1 returned -0.3% against +8.8% for static.
   - 2011 (proxy era): the only episode where L1 cut the drawdown, 12.8% against 15.3%, and it still returned less.
7. **H42 did no harm and no good.** Ending wealth was within about 1.4% of static in every window. It held no cash at the March 2020 or June 2022 troughs because the trend state turns OFF after the fall. Its 15% drawdown-reduction pass threshold is not met.
8. **DHHF is a close substitute.** Since Dec 2020 it returned 11.55% time-weighted against 12.34% for the two-fund plan. At IBKR, A$6.60 on each A$500 order cost A$497, about 1.2% of money paid in.
9. **Owner-plan band:** steering all contributions to VAS from Nov 2024 still left Australia at 34.5%. Above roughly A$150,000, A$500 a month can no longer hold the 35–45% band without selling.

## Method and assumptions

- **Timing:** month-end votes per PRT-12/13; execution at the close of the first trading day of the next month.
- **IBKR costs:** max(A$6.60, 0.088%) plus 5 bp half-spread, whole units, A$1,000 minimum (A$500 for transition buys), ±20% band.
- **A$0 platform:** fractional units, 5 bp half-spread, reinvestment on pay date.
- **H42:** run per report 35's card (cash waits if no sleeve is ON). Your wording ("else to the underweight sleeve") is run as a variant; it makes no material difference.
- **Owner plan:** contribution kept flat at A$500 (the plan's 3% yearly rise is not applied).
- **Tax:** 39% marginal, actual Vanguard franking and foreign-tax components, FIFO parcels, 50% discount after 12 months, losses carried forward, tax paid from outside the account, full sale on the last day.
- **Not modelled:** sentinel unit, A$50 reserve, tranches, tick slippage, T+2 on band proceeds, historical fee schedules. The AAA variant ignores AAA dealing costs, so it is an upper bound.

## Caveats

- One source for ASX closes; no daily second source before Oct 2014.
- One 12–17 year sample with no slow bear market. This confirms what the rules cost while waiting; it does not contradict 36's 2000–02 and 2007–09 finding.
- Not a registered card run; the 2024+ holdout is used.
- A$5,000-start time-weighted returns are sensitive to the first months. H42's higher time-weighted return from 2009 is such a start-date accident; the A$50,000 rows are the cleaner comparison.
