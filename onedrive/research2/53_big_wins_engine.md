What this means, in one paragraph. The steady-winners rule is the best company-level rule the programme has found, and it is real enough to have beaten all, or all but one, of 200 random portfolios over 23 years. But the whole advantage was earned before 2016. In the eight years the rule had not been tuned on, it was level with or behind the index before dividends and behind it after dividends and tax, and every attempt to add a selling rule made it worse when tested on unseen years. That is report 48's and report 51's finding a third time. The honest use of this engine is the one the owner has already chosen: run it on the IBKR paper account, score it every month against the index, keep real money in A200 and BGBL, and treat any later real-money slice as a small, capped hobby position the owner chooses knowingly, not as something the evidence recommends.

## 0.1 Caveats that change how far to trust this

- **The "unseen" period is not fully unseen.** Report 51 printed the yearly results of this score for every cohort from 2000 to 2023, and I read them before designing anything. The 2016–May 2024 period is blind for the new mechanics (sell rules, account sizes, costs, tax, the industry cap), not for the idea. The only truly unseen data are dated after 14 November 2026.
- **Same data as report 51, same defects.** Unlicensed third-party prices, about one in five splits of delisted companies probably missed, demergers mostly unadjusted before 2016, no dividends, delistings paid at the last price (failures at zero). Report 51 sections 0.1 and 11 apply in full.
- **Price-only main results.** Stock and index are both shown without dividends. The dividend estimate (Table 4.5) covers 2016–2024 only and comes from companies still listed in 2026.
- **Tax is approximate.** 32% on net realised gains each financial year; regime A has the 50% discount for lots held 12 months, regime B has none (the stand-in for the rules from 1 July 2027; indexation ignored, so B is a little too harsh).
- **Industry groups are today's.** The 25% industry cap uses the ASX company list of 14 November 2026. Companies that have since delisted have no group and are never capped, so the back-test understates how often the cap would bind (for example on the property trusts of 2007).
- **Execution is idealised.** Sells and buys at the next day's close, same day. The spec's live rules are limit orders from 11:30 and buys only from settled cash. A two-day settlement delay and doubled costs are in Table 4.6.
- **Small accounts are path-dependent.** A 1% change in the starting balance moved the 23-year result by up to 0.15 points a year; settlement timing moved the A$25,000 unseen-period result by 0.8 points. Differences of that size between rows are noise.
- **No web access to the data vendor in this session** (`eodhd.com` is outside the session's allowed network). Vendor prices and terms are taken from reports 19 and 52 and the spec, and are marked [unverified here].

## 2. Engine design v0 (written before any run; frozen unchanged as v-final)

The exact text a validator works from is `out/FINAL_RULES.md`. This section gives each choice and its reason. Nothing here was tuned.

### 2.1 The rule

| Element | Choice | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | Companies with at least 200 traded days in the last 252, the 300 with the highest median daily traded value. No ETFs or other exchange-traded products. Property trusts and listed investment companies stay in | Report 51's pre-registered universe. Below the top 300 the half-tick alone can be several percent |
| Liquidity floor | A name can be bought only if its median daily traded value is at least 100 times the position size (the spec's 1%-of-volume rule, RSK-07) and its price is at least A$0.50 (half a tick is then at most 0.5%) | Report 51 Table 6.1: momentum's cost drag was 5.5 points a year where picks were sub-10-cent stocks |
| Signal | H80 exactly as pre-registered: the mean of four percentile ranks inside the 300: (a) price one month ago ÷ price 12 months ago; (b) price ÷ price 6 months ago; (c) price ÷ highest close of the last 252 days; (d) minus the standard deviation of daily log returns over 252 days (at least 200 observations). All four required | Report 51: rank IC +0.16 (t = 3.6), positive in 83% of years; top tenth +4.9 points a year (t = 1.9); 3–4% of its picks lost more than half, against 20% for plain momentum. `code/t1.py` confirms my scores equal report 51's to the last digit |
| Number of holdings | n = min(30, max(4, floor(account value ÷ A$2,500))): 10 names at A$25,000, 30 from A$75,000 | A$6.60 is 0.26% of A$2,500 a side; at A$1,000 it is 0.66%. Report 51: the information is spread over about 30 names; a 4-stock list has a luck band 17 points a year wide. 30 is H80's tenth |
| Weighting | Equal dollars at purchase. Winners are left alone until a holding is more than twice the target size, then trimmed back to twice | No evidence supports a cleverer weight; "let winners run" within a 2× cap (6.7% of the account at 30 names, 20% at 10) |
| Entry | Decision at a month-end close, trade at the next day's close | Report 51's timing; nothing after the decision day enters a signal |
| Review frequency | Once a year, last trading day of December. The first month-end of an account is an initial formation | H80 as registered. Report 51: momentum lists keep only 2–5% of their names from one year to the next, so every extra review is extra brokerage and tax; this engine's measured turnover is already 76–99% a year. June cohorts agree in sign. More frequent reviews were round 2 of the loop, which was never reached |
| Sell rule | A holding that is not in the new December list is sold. Nothing is sold between reviews | The simplest reading of H80. Eight alternatives were tested in round 1 (section 3) |
| Loss-cut | None | Tested: three stop-loss rules and a relative loss-cut did not help on 2001–2015 (Table 3.2) |
| Position cap | 2× target at a review (trim), nothing between reviews | Above |
| Industry cap | At most a quarter of the names (3 of 10, 8 of 30) from one GICS industry group | Report 51 Table 8.8: in 2007 the low-volatility model's calmest stocks included three Rubicon property trusts that lost 96–99%. Not testable properly on this data (section 0.1) |
| Turnover limit | None in the rule; the yearly review bounds it at about 100% a year. A monitor warns above 150% (BW-21) | Measured turnover 76–99% a year |
| Tax-aware rule | A sale at a review of a holding that is in profit and within 31 days of passing the 12-month test is delayed to the first day it passes (367 calendar days after purchase). Losses are sold at once | A January purchase and a sale on the first trading day of the next January are 365 days apart, which fails the 12-month test. With the rule, 95–97% of profitable review sales were long-term (`code/t1.py`) |
| Market-regime guard | None | Report 51: the crash-filtered momentum model (M14) was no better than plain momentum; the binary trend filter added 1.7 points (t = 1.1). The guard was round 4 of the loop, never reached. The round-1 trend exit, its closest relative, failed on unseen data |
| Vacancies | If a holding leaves between reviews (takeover, delisting), the cash buys the best-scored eligible name not held at the next month-end | Otherwise takeover cash sits idle for up to 11 months at 0% |
| Delisting and halts | A holding with no trade for 20 trading days that never trades again is paid out at its last price, or at zero if ASX's reason is a failure (administration, liquidation, unpaid fees). A suspended stock that later resumes is held at its last price. An order for a stock that does not trade that day is carried (sell) or dropped (buy) | Report 51's convention with failures at zero. In the 23-year run 37 of 608 positions (6%) left this way |
| Corporate actions | Prices are adjusted for splits and consolidations; dividends go to cash; a demerged company is held to the next review and then treated like any holding; rights are not taken up; no dividend reinvestment plan | Live handling in BW-17 |

### 2.2 Costs and tax used throughout

Commission max(A$6.60, 0.088%). Half-spread per side: 0.10% for the 100 most-traded names, 0.25% for ranks 101–300, 0.50% outside; never less than half a tick. Whole shares. Idle cash at 0%. Tax at 32% on net realised gains per financial year, reserved in cash as gains are realised and paid each June; regime A with the 12-month discount, regime B without. The index is bought once and taxed once at the end.

## 3. The bounded improvement loop

### 3.1 Rules declared before starting (`PREREG_BW.md`, hashed)

- **Budget:** at most 5 rounds × 8 variants = 40 variants, plus v0 = 41 rule versions; at most 6 looks at the unseen period (one for v0, one per round).
- **Development data:** decisions from December 2000 to November 2015. **Unseen ("holdout") data:** a fresh account from December 2015 to 31 May 2024, the last date with every delisted company present.
- **All five rounds and all 36 variants were written down before the first run:** round 1 sell rules; round 2 review frequency and buffers; round 3 weights, number of names, universe size, caps; round 4 market-regime guards; round 5 changes inside the signal.
- **Per round:** run the variants on development data only, at A$25,000 and A$100,000. A variant is a candidate if it beats the current engine at both sizes after costs and tax (regime B), by at least 0.25 points a year on average, without a drawdown more than 5 points deeper. The best candidate, and only that one, is run once on the unseen period.
- **Adoption** needs a higher after-tax return than the current engine at both sizes on the unseen period, **and** three luck controls there: at or above the 90th percentile of 200 random portfolios run through the identical machinery; a paired bootstrap of the monthly difference (and, for a sell rule, the 90th percentile of 100 versions that sell at random with the same frequency); and a deflated Sharpe ratio of at least 0.95 counting all 303 trials of report 51 plus this loop's.
- **Stop** at the first look that shows no improvement. Nothing is ever chosen on the unseen period.

What the loop teaches, beyond "stop":

1. **A 1.6-point gain on 15 development years (the variant's monthly excess over the index had t = 2.4 to 3.0 there) reversed on the next 8.** The development result was not a bug; the rule fitted 2001–2003 and 2008.
2. **Stop-losses did not help even in development.** They sell the volatile part of a list chosen for low volatility, pay the spread twice and realise short-term gains or losses.
3. **v0 also fails the luck controls on the unseen period** (37th and 69th percentile of random portfolios; DSR 0.05). Had v0 been a "variant", it would not have been adopted either.

### 3.5 Deviations from the pre-registration

- The first run of look 2 computed its numbers and crashed while saving them (a data-type error in the log writer). The log writer was fixed and the look recomputed; the engine is deterministic and the numbers are identical to the row the crashed run had already logged. One look, counted once.
- After the freeze, as section 6 of the pre-registration allows: an engine check against report 51's cohort arithmetic (mean absolute difference 0.5 points a year over 23 one-year runs, within 0.15 points in 18 of them; the larger gaps come from rules the cohort arithmetic lacks); two execution sensitivities added as options that default to off (v0's development result unchanged to six decimals); reporting-only runs at A$5,000 and A$10,000.

## 6. The owner-picked slice

### 6.1 Design in one paragraph

The owner may buy companies he chooses, with new money, up to a small cap. **The system never places these orders, in any mode.** It is the record-keeper and the honest scorer: it checks a proposed pick against the caps before the trade, makes the owner write down why before he knows the outcome, keeps the tax parcels, and every month prints the picks beside what the same dollars would have earned in the index on the same days. Paper picks (no money) are allowed from the first day and are scored the same way, which is the cheap way to find out whether the picks are any good.

### 6.2 Rules

| Rule | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Total cap | Cost of open picks ≤ 10% of (index account + picks + any Big Wins slice), checked at each purchase | Owner decision. Small enough to lose entirely (report 51, learning 13) |
| Per-company cap | ≤ 2% of that total at purchase (config allows up to 3%) | One failure cannot move the plan |
| Growth | A pick that grows past its cap is not force-sold. Alert when one company passes 5% or the slice passes 15% by market value; no further buying of that name or of any pick while above | "Let winners run" without letting the slice take over |
| Source of money | New contributions only. Never by selling A200 or BGBL | Section 1.1 of the spec: no selling, no own picks from the index account |
| Instruments | ASX-listed ordinary shares. No margin, no short sales, no options, warrants, CFDs, leveraged or inverse products | Owner decision ("never margin") |
| Waiting time | 48 hours between the logged proposal and the trade | The same pause the index account already has for sales; removes tips acted on in the hour |
| Reason log | Before each buy: at least 30 words on why; what would prove it wrong; the horizon in months; where the idea came from (own work, tip, news, social media); the expected result against the index. Before each sell: why. Stored with a timestamp, never edited | A prediction that is written down can be scored. One that is not gets remembered kindly |
| Unlogged trades | A buy entered with no proposal on file is accepted (the system cannot stop it) and marked `UNLOGGED_PICK` in every later report | The record must stay complete |
| Stop-loss | Optional alerts only (for example 20% below cost, or the owner's own "proved wrong" price). An alert is a message, never an order | Round 1 of the loop: mechanical stop-losses did not help. An alert buys attention, not return |
| Tax | Parcel ledger; the 12-month date of each parcel; an estimate of the tax before any proposed sale; the financial-year report | Section 13 engine |

**Feasibility at small totals.** At a total of A$25,000 the 2% cap is A$500 a company, where A$6.60 is 1.3% a side; the 10% slice is five such positions. The pre-trade check prints the brokerage as a percentage and warns above 1% (below A$660 an order). Below a total of about A$33,000 every 2% pick carries that warning.

### 6.3 What the system does

| Role | How |
|---|---|
| Track | A third book, `ledger_<user>_picks`, fed by hand entry or an imported statement (the pattern of SYS-34). Never bound to a broker; never holds an order row |
| Cap | `trader picks propose` runs the cap, instrument and waiting-time checks and answers ALLOW or REFUSE with a reason code. After the trade, a breach is a standing line in the report |
| Score | For every pick and for the slice: money-weighted return after brokerage; the **index twin** (the same dollars into A200 total return, and into 40/60, on the same days, sold on the same days); the difference in dollars and in points a year; after estimated tax. Closed picks stay in the table for ever |
| Luck band | The slice against 1,000 sets of random top-300 stocks bought and sold on the same dates in the same amounts: the percentile, with the plain statement of what it can and cannot show |
| Thesis check | At each pick's horizon: what was predicted, what happened, whether the "proved wrong" condition occurred and whether the owner acted on it |
| Tax ledger | Parcels, 12-month dates, realised gains and losses, carry-forward, the yearly report |

### 6.4 The report: "Your picks, honestly" (monthly, one page, fixed order)

1. One sentence first: "Since [date] your picks are A$X ahead of / behind the same money in A200" (and against 40/60).
2. The table of every pick, open and closed, **in order of purchase date, never sorted by result**: date, company, cost, value or proceeds, return, index twin's return, difference, reason in the owner's first ten words, status.
3. Counts: picks that beat their index twin; picks that lost more than half; the share of all profit that came from the single best pick.
4. The luck band percentile, and until there are at least 20 closed picks or 36 months: "Too early to tell skill from luck. With ten stocks, luck alone moves the average by about ±11 points a year."
5. Thesis checks that fell due this month.
6. Caps: used and remaining; any breach; any `UNLOGGED_PICK`.
7. Tax: unrealised gain, the next 12-month dates, realised this financial year.
8. At each dated review (OPS-20): if there are at least 10 picks over at least 24 months and the slice is more than 10% behind its index twin in dollars, the report says so and proposes halving the cap. The owner decides in writing either way and the decision is logged (MON-17).

The report never shows a pick without its index twin, never drops a closed loser, and never calls a result skill.

## 7. What a forward paper record can and cannot prove, and what follows for real money

**The arithmetic.** The engine's yearly return differs from the index's by about 8.5 points (standard deviation; 8.3 at A$100,000 and 9.2 at A$25,000 over 23 years, 12.2 at A$25,000 in 2016–2024). Suppose its true edge were +2.5 points a year after costs, which is its whole-period result and more than the unseen period showed.

| Evidence standard | Years of record needed to expect to reach it |
|---|---|
| The spec's return bar, t ≥ 3.0 (RND-001) | about 104 |
| Report 52's rising bar at 312 trials, t ≥ 3.77 (LE-23) | about 164 |
| Ordinary one-sided 95% (t ≥ 1.645) | about 31 |

So "a forward paper record proves it" cannot mean statistical proof on any horizon the owner cares about. What a paper record **can** show in three years: that the engine is built as specified (the paper book matches the replay); what it really costs to trade; that it has not blown up; and how it felt to watch it lag in the years it lags. That is worth having.

**A promotion test that states its own error rates.** If the owner still wants a rule for the capped slice, the least dishonest one is a confidence test read only at fixed dates (report 52's 36, 60 and 120 months), with the chance of a lucky pass stated. With a total allowance of 20% for a lucky pass, split across the three looks:

| Look | Engine must be ahead of A200, after costs and tax, by at least | Chance of passing if the engine has no edge | Chance of passing if the true edge is +2.5 pts/yr | …if +5 pts/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 36 months | 7.4 points a year | 6.7% | 16% | 32% |
| 60 months | 5.7 points a year | 6.7% | 20% | 43% |
| 120 months | 4.0 points a year | 6.7% | 28% | 64% |

[computed from a tracking difference of 8.5% a year; normal approximation]

**Honest forecast.** The most likely outcome is that the paper engine roughly matches the index before dividends, lags it somewhat after dividends and tax, and never passes this test. That is the same forecast section 17.1 of the spec makes for the main system, and it is a good outcome: the money is in the index throughout. If the engine does pass at 36 months it will most likely be luck (a true edge of +2.5 passes 16% of the time; no edge passes 7%), which is why the slice stays capped, why passing gives no return claim, and why the demotion rule (BW-29) matters as much as the promotion rule.

**Earliest dates.** v2.14 cannot be admitted before about mid-January 2027 (OPS-19 (a)); the 90-day cooling-off for a new instrument type runs to 12 February 2027 (OPS-19 (d)); first paper formation at the month-end of Friday 26 February 2027, first paper orders Monday 1 March 2027; first formal look after the February 2030 month-end; earliest real-money slice, after the 7-day and 90-day waits, about mid-2030.

## 10. Reasons this could be wrong

1. **The data.** Missed splits and unadjusted demergers add noise; failures paid at zero only where ASX's reason says so. A licensed re-run could move every number by a point or more in either direction. That is why H82 requires it.
2. **The unseen period is one eight-year stretch** with two fast V-shaped falls (2020, 2022) that punish slow and trend-based rules alike. A different eight years could have favoured the trend exit. The loop's answer would then have been different, and equally uncertain.
3. **Stopping at round 1 left the owner's other ideas untested**: review frequency, number of names, regime guards. I followed the declared stop rule. It is possible that a quarterly review or a wider list is better; nothing here says so or denies it (card H86).
4. **Prices only.** The dividend estimate is from survivors over eight years. If the engine's names pay more than estimated, Table 4.5 is too harsh; if franking is counted, the index gains about a point (report 39) and the engine somewhat less or more depending on its names.
5. **Regime B overstates future tax** by ignoring indexation; regime A will not apply to gains after 1 July 2027. The truth for a live slice lies between the two after-tax columns.
6. **The industry cap could not be tested where it matters** (2007), because delisted companies have no industry group in this data.
7. **The promotion test rests on a normal approximation** and a tracking difference measured in the past. If the engine's gap to the index is more volatile in future, the required margins are too low.
8. **My own exposure.** I read report 51's results before designing the engine and its variants. The pre-registration limits what I could do after seeing new numbers; it cannot remove what I already knew.

## 11. What the owner can take from this

1. **The engine is built to the standard asked for:** rules frozen, hashed, written so someone else can rebuild them blind, tested on years it was not designed on, with every attempt logged.
2. **"Keep making it better" has a limit that showed up in round one.** The change that looked best in development was the worst thing to do on unseen data. More rounds would have produced more of those, not fewer.
3. **What it is good for:** a monthly, like-for-like answer to "what would a careful stock-picking rule have done with this money?", beside the index, on paper.
4. **What it is not:** a way to own the next NEXTDC. It held NEXTDC for one flat year and one good half-year in twelve.
5. **If a real slice is ever wanted,** A$25,000 is the smallest that is an engine and not a punt, and it should be signed for as a choice (statement C), not as a result.
6. **The owner's own picks get the same treatment:** written reason first, 48 hours, small caps, and the index twin printed beside every pick for ever.
