## Summary
- What the famous quant firms do cannot be copied: it needs speed, borrowing and paid data, and their public funds are ordinary.
- Published retail algorithms do not hold up. In one study of 888 of them, back-test results explained 1-2% of live results.
- No share-picking rule passed: insider buying, earnings drift, low volatility and a 40-share home-made index all failed to clearly beat the fund.
- None of 13 packaged US funds (small value, quality, covered calls, managed futures and more) beat the S&P 500 fund in both halves of its life.
- Tax structure is the biggest lever. Super is worth 1.0-1.8 points a year over holding personally. A company is no better than personal holding.
- Fast trading is ruinous at the top tax rate: one rule earned 14.8% a year before tax and 8.2% after it.
- One rule met the pass mark on paper: a triple-leveraged S&P 500 fund held only in uptrends. It is not recommended: it fell 61% and lost 56% in 2022.

## What it means for you
The expected gain from any strategy tested, over the index fund, is zero, with deeper falls. No company and no self-managed fund is needed. Hold the index fund, put as much of it as the rules allow inside your existing super fund's index option, hold for more than 12 months, and do not trade it.

## How sure are we?
The tax comparison is arithmetic on the same fund, so it is solid. On strategies: the best result (the leveraged rule, t 2.1) is below the luck bar (3.6), and its lead is borrowing, not skill. The insider test here was short (2018-2026); it got its own study next.
