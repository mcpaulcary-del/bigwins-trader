## Summary
- Running Big Wins costs about $835-1,260 a year (server, price data, brokerage). On $10,000 that is 8-12%. The A200 fund costs $4.
- The costs are nearly flat, so they shrink as a share of a bigger account: about 4% at $25,000, 2% at $50,000, 1% at $100,000.
- The believable future edge, from the project's own record, is +1 to +3% a year, and zero is possible. The strategy lost to the fund from 2002 to 2009.
- No paper period can prove an edge this small. The forward record can only catch large failures.
- Stay with the current broker: it is the only one with a mature way for a server to place ASX orders and real paper trading.
- The price data is already near the cheapest licensed option. Paying yearly saves about $60.
- Small-company funds run by professionals: 60% lagged the index over 15 years, and they charge about 1%, not 10%.

## What it means for you
The $10,000 is the price of an experiment, not a wealth plan. Set a written budget, keep all other long-term money in an index fund, and keep Big Wins to 10-20% of your investments at most. Do not add money until at least 18-24 months of forward record shows it ahead of both its random twins and A200 after all costs.

## How sure are we?
The costs are facts. The chance of beating the fund is the reviewer's judgement, not a measurement: about 1 in 3 in any single year at $10,000, by luck rather than edge; perhaps 35-40% at $50,000. Some broker and data prices could not be checked on the providers' own pages and are marked to verify before acting.
