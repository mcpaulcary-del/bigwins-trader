## Summary
- From 2000 to 2026 the S&P 500 fund turned $50,000 into about $231,000 after tax (6.0% a year). The best momentum book reached about $263,000 (6.4%).
- That 0.4-point lead is well inside luck, and the book was behind the fund for 2000-2012.
- The direct copy of Big Wins (US ranks 501-1500) reached about $96,000 (2.4% a year), worse than 97 of 100 random books.
- Costs are not the problem in the US: 0.1-0.3% of the account a year, even at $10,000.
- Tax is the problem. The fastest rule beat the fund by about 3 points a year before tax in 2013-2026 and lost by 3 points after tax.
- With $650 a year of running costs added, every rule is behind the fund at every size.
- The falls were bigger than the fund's: 61-87% against 56%.

## What it means for you
There is no US version of Big Wins worth running. No US paper line was started. An index fund puts off nearly all its tax for years; a trading rule pays it every year. Hold a global or S&P 500 index fund as your core.

## How sure are we?
Sure nothing passed: the best lead (t 1.0) is far below the luck bar (3.6), and nothing passed in either half. The US price history is messy before about 2015, so 2000-2012 is the less reliable half; the conclusion does not depend on it.
