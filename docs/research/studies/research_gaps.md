## Summary
- The share-picking score is real. The top-scored fifth beat the rest of ranks 301-600 by 2.09 points a month before costs, in both halves of the history.
- The $10,000 book built on it is weak. There were fewer than 18 buyable shares in 56 of 105 months since 2018, and without the best 5% of picks the result is behind.
- The old headline ($34,520) let the account grow without limit. The live trader caps the book at $10,000 and leaves the rest in cash.
- In 2002-17 the same live rules ended at $19,710 against $31,689 for the index fund.
- Two faults were found: a data-gap rule (rule 14) is checked before sales, so thin data could block a sale; and cash above the cap sits idle.
- The lottery filter removes 46% of the top fifth with no measured benefit.
- Price-only ideas from the academic literature added nothing.
