## Summary
- On paper it beat the index over 2004-2017: 8.5% a year after tax against 6.1% held personally, and 10.4% against 7.5% in super.
- All of that lead came from 2004-2010. In 2011-2017 it matched the fund: 15.4% against 15.5%.
- The years 2018-2026 had already shown nothing. So the edge has been absent for about 16 years, which fits an effect that faded after it was published in 2012.
- The data flatters the rule: it mostly covers companies that still exist. After removing that tilt the lead is 0.8 points personally.
- It missed three of the six pass marks fixed in advance.
- The falls were deeper than the fund's: 63% at worst against 39%.

## What it means for you
No insider-copying line will be run. This was the last open US idea. Keep to the advice of the wider US study: your index fund, as much as allowed inside super, held more than 12 months.

## How sure are we?
The test was fixed in advance and run once on unseen years, and it failed its own pass marks (t 1.7 against a pass mark of 2.0 and a luck bar of 3.6). The rule did beat 99 of 100 random books overall, a real sign of information in 2004-2010, but not since.
